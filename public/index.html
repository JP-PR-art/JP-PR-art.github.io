Internal tooling / sales operations

An agentic sales-ops system where the issue tracker is the CRM

Context
CPQ software vendor, internal build
Role
Product owner and sole builder
Stack
Claude, Jira API, MCP, cron agents

Challenge

The pipeline lived in scattered notes and inboxes. Sourcing, reply triage, and follow-up were manual, and nothing an agent did would be auditable enough to trust.

Approach

I made the issue tracker the source of truth and ran cron-driven agents on top of it: a daily prospect sourcer, a reply classifier reading live email over MCP, and a drafting agent, each writing behind a human approval gate with full audit logging and ICP-based prioritization.

Outcome

Ran in production for weeks. Daily sourcing and reply classification automated, every write logged, nothing sent to a real person without a human in the loop.

02
Method / agent engineering

A multi-agent delivery pipeline with a human approval gate

Challenge

Most agentic coding stops at "the model wrote something." Real delivery needs a plan, a build, tests, a review, and a human decision before anything ships.

Approach

Four specialized agents (planner, coder, tester, reviewer) behind one API, with a restart-safe approval gate between plan and execution. Pending approvals persist to disk, so killing and restarting the server never loses or double-runs work.

Outcome

A one-command demo reproducible from a clean clone, the whole pipeline and approval path exercised end to end, and a codebase hardened after its own review.

03
LLM reliability / delivery

Bug-vs-change triage, proven before it was trusted

Context
Delivery and contract scope
Role
Designed the evaluation method
Domain
LLM reliability

Challenge

Deciding whether client feedback is a bug (covered) or new scope (billable) is high-stakes and easy to get wrong. I would not put an LLM on that call without proof.

Approach

Blind, pre-registered evaluation waves. A first wave with known outcomes exposed the failure modes; I root-caused them, added fixes and a "needs more info" verdict class, then ran a held-out, pre-registered second wave behind a go/no-go gate.

Outcome

Failure modes caught before any live use. The system only advances on held-out performance, never on the strength of a good demo.

04
SMB delivery / in build

Replacing a construction company's Excel weekplanning

Challenge

The weekly crew and crane plan lived in Excel, then got printed on A4 for the crews. Pen edits on the paper, a swapped shift or a delayed site, never made it back into the file, so the two copies drifted apart during the week.

Approach

A single-planner web app: a weekly crew grid, a crane-fleet view, and the same PDF, PNG, and Excel export and WhatsApp handoff the company already uses. Excel stays a first-class import and export format, so the spreadsheet is still valid if the app is ever unavailable, and a conflict engine flags a double-booked crane the moment it happens.

Outcome

In build. My estimate, not audited: building and eyeballing a week by hand took on the order of an hour, and a double-booking was easy to miss. Nine phases were sequenced on a public status page before a line of production code.
